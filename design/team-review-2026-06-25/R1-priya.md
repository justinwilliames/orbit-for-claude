# R1 — Priya Iyer (Chief of Staff / Ops)

**Lens:** operational / leverage / what's-not-being-said. Not visual taste.

## Verdict
We are about to spend a 7-person review polishing 1 of 24 emails while nobody in this room can tell me whether the creative moves the recovery rate at all — and the headline we're debating is a 12× decision being made like a 1× one. Ship the program, instrument it, then earn the right to polish.

## Top 3 findings

**1. The headline is a 12× decision and it has no owner.** Everyone's treating "Your payment didn't go through" as one email's headline. It isn't. CONTEXT says this treatment gets **replicated across 12 Marketing variants**. So the call on type/scale/weight/placement is a template-system decision, not a creative-taste decision — get it wrong and you've shipped the same wrongness twelve times, then have to re-touch twelve templates. That's the leverage point of this entire review and it's the one thing being discussed as if it's local. Whoever owns the headline pattern owns it for the program, not for Day-0. Right now that owner is "the room," which means it's nobody. **Owner by Friday: Justin makes the headline-pattern call, explicitly, as a system rule — not a per-email vibe.** Side note worth ten seconds, not ten people: the copy is 5 words against a 4-word hero hard-rule. That's a brand-rule breach hiding under an aesthetics debate. Decide if the rule bends for dunning or the copy cuts to 4 — but decide it once, for all 12.

**2. The assumption nobody's stating: "the creative matters to recovery rate."** This is dunning. Recovery is driven far more by *send timing, retry cadence, deliverability into the inbox, and CTA-to-billing-flow friction* than by whether the H1 is regular or bold weight. We have zero measurement plan in the brief. If we polish the headline and recovery moves, we won't know it was the headline. If it doesn't move, we'll blame the creative and re-polish. **No control, no readout, no attribution = we're spending senior time on an input we can't measure.** That's the awkward question for the room: if I told you the headline change buys +0.0% recovery, would you still spend three rounds on it?

**3. The real risk lives in the other 23 emails and the Braze wiring, not this pixel.** One email is inspectable on a viewstripo link. Where are the other 11 Marketing + 12 Transactional? Are the Transactional ones (the ones that *legally must* land — receipt-adjacent, deliverability-critical) even drafted? A dunning *program* fails on the seams: the Day-3/Day-7 escalation copy, the "card updated, you're sorted" success state, the suppression logic so a recovered customer doesn't keep getting dunned. **That's where churn-dollars leak — not in whether the hero is centred.** We're inspecting the prettiest tile and calling it the program.

## The single thing I'd ship
**This Day-0 email, today, as-is** — once the headline is cut/approved to satisfy the 4-word rule (or the rule is consciously waived for dunning). It's calm, on-brand, credible, CTA is clear. It is *good enough to recover money*. A bounced card is an active wound; the cost of waiting a week to perfect the H1 weight is real lost MRR. Ship, measure, iterate on data.

## What I'd defer
- The headline weight/scale/placement *aesthetic* debate → **defer to a single Justin call applied across all 12 Marketing templates at once**, not litigated per-email across rounds.
- Footer spacing polish → defer; it's raw Liquid in preview anyway, near-zero recovery impact.
- Any "headline-above-image vs below" structural redesign → defer unless someone shows it changes click-to-billing. Otherwise it's taste dressed as strategy.

## One question for the orchestrator
Before Round 2 burns more cycles: **what's the recovery-rate measurement plan, and are the other 23 emails drafted or not?** If the answer is "no plan / not drafted," then the highest-value output of this review isn't a prettier headline — it's a one-page program ship-checklist with an owner per email and a control cell in Braze. Tell me which review we're actually running.

— Priya

# R1 — Devi Sharma · Conversion & Motivation

**Role:** Growth / Product Marketing · dunning recovery lens
**Template:** Sophiie "Safety Net" Day-0 failed-payment email
**Date:** 2026-06-25

---

## Verdict

The email is calm and credible — which is correct — but it leads with the problem instead of the outcome, and a paying trades customer who bounces off the headline without reading the body will feel *told* rather than *moved*.

---

## Top 3 Findings

### 1. The headline is an error report, not a recovery signal

"Your payment didn't go through" is factually accurate and emotionally flat. It announces a problem without hinting that the fix is fast, easy, or worth doing now. For a trades operator opening email between jobs, on a phone, in 4 seconds, the headline is the entire message — and this one stops at the bad news.

The body copy does the motivational work that the headline should be doing: "A quick update keeps Sophiie answering your calls, covering your after-hours, and managing your inbox." That is the story. That is what makes the card update feel worth 2 minutes. The headline buries it.

Compound problem: the brand rule caps hero H1 at 4 words / 1 sentence. This headline is 5 words and runs two lines on desktop. It is both over-length and under-converting. One problem solves the other — shorten it to something that leads with continuity, not failure.

Suggested direction (4 words, outcome-forward, calm): "Keep Sophiie on duty." or "Don't lose your cover." — both stay calm, stay brand-register, and plant the loss-frame before the body lands it. The exact line is a copy decision; the direction is structural.

### 2. Hero image is doing brand work, not conversion work

The tradeswoman photo is on-brand and warm. It is also doing zero motivational lifting. In a dunning email, the hero image is one of very few chances to remind the customer what they are paying for — what is at stake if they don't act. A real human answering a phone, a van dispatched, a job booked: any of those would reinforce "Sophiie is working for you right now, and this is what stops if the payment stays failed." The current image is a desk scene that reads more like an onboarding email than a recovery one. It does not add urgency or stakes. It is pleasant, and pleasant is not the conversion driver here.

This is the one place a subtle shift — keeping the same photoreal, documentary, AU-trades style — could make the visual stack earn its space.

### 3. The 2-minute friction-reducer is doing its job but is buried in line 3 of the body

"It only takes two minutes" is the single most conversion-relevant piece of copy in the email. It directly addresses the #1 reason a busy tradie defers action: perceived effort. It is currently the last line of the body, after the value proposition, after the reason-to-act. It should be closer to the CTA — either as the final line immediately above the button, or as a subtext directly below the button label ("Takes less than 2 minutes"). Right now a fast scroller goes: logo → headline → image → CTA, and never sees it.

---

## The Single Thing I'd Ship

Rewrite the headline. Four words, outcome-forward, inside brand voice. Move the loss-frame (what stops working) to the headline, and let the body copy substantiate it. This is a one-field change in Stripo that changes what 100% of openers read, on every send, for the full dunning sequence.

The body copy is already good. The CTA label is already right — "Update payment details" is specific, low-anxiety, and accurate. Do not change either.

---

## What I'd Defer

The hero image swap. It would help conversion, but it requires a new brief, a new shoot or sourcing pass, and brand sign-off. The ROI on the headline rewrite comes in the next send cycle. The hero image is a second-cycle improvement.

Also deferring any changes to the body copy structure — it reads clearly and the value proposition is intact. Fixing the friction-reducer placement (finding 3) is a fast follow, but the headline is the gate.
